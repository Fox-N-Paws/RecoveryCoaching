Why Coaching?

Coaching can really help the individual who is in the contemplative or preparation stage of change.

Going into treatment is a huge commitment and can cause a great deal of anxiety for people who are afraid they will lose their job, or mothers having to leave their children. Coaching can be the bridge that helps make the decision if treatment is necessary.

Also, A.A. can be intimidating. Atheists and agnostics can struggle with religious concepts within the program. I am able to clearly explain the freedom that an individual can find in fitting their personal belief system into the spiritual principles of AA.

If AA is not a fit for the individual, I believe there are other successful roads to recovery by using CBT and spiritual concepts.

For Family Members

Loving someone with an alcohol or substance use problem takes its own toll. Drawing on Al-Anon principles and decades of experience working alongside families in treatment, I offer coaching specifically for spouses, parents, adult children, and other loved ones who need support of their own.

  • Understanding addiction as a disease rather than a choice or a failure of willpower
  • Letting go of guilt, shame, and the urge to control someone else's drinking
  • Setting and holding healthy boundaries with compassion
  • Learning the difference between helping and enabling
  • Rebuilding trust and communication if your loved one is in recovery
  • Finding your own support, whether or not your loved one ever gets help

This support is available whether your loved one is in treatment, resistant to it, or already in recovery. Learn more about Al-Anon and who qualifies on the For Families page.

For Families & Loved Ones

“You Didn’t Cause It, You Can’t Control It, and You Can’t Cure It.”

Addiction affects the entire family. Loving someone who is struggling with alcohol or drugs can mean living with constant worry, walking on eggshells, trying to prevent consequences, or becoming so focused on their choices that you begin to lose your own boundaries and sense of stability.

🐾 Understanding Codependency & Boundaries

Behaviors sometimes called “enabling” often begin as attempts to help, protect, or survive. Family coaching can help you recognize the difference between helping and rescuing, supporting and enabling, compassion and taking responsibility.

A boundary is about what you will do—not about controlling another person.

🐾 Your Recovery Matters, Too

You do not have to wait for your loved one to seek help before beginning your own recovery. Support may include Al-Anon, Nar-Anon, family recovery groups, therapy, individual coaching, journaling, healthy friendships, and learning to set boundaries.

What Families Need to Know

Things Every Family Should Know

Loving someone with an addiction does not mean taking responsibility for their recovery.

  • Addiction is not caused by a lack of love.
  • You cannot love someone into sobriety.
  • Setting a boundary is not being cruel.
  • Detaching does not mean you stop loving someone.
  • You do not have to wait for your loved one to change before changing your own life.
  • You are allowed to have a life while someone you love is struggling.
  • Your loved one’s addiction is not your failure.
When addiction enters a family, the whole family needs support. Recovery isn’t only for the person struggling with substances—it can be for the people who love them, too.

Why the Dogs?

Three generations of dogs have worked with me in treatment. Clients requested the dogs attend groups, conjoint and individual sessions — and even their graduating ceremonies.

I was informed that the dogs helped reduce their anxiety and depression and made them feel happy, and added a "normal" feeling to a very challenging and scary time in their lives.

The Science

Studies have proven the powerful gifts these animals can have in reducing the psychological stress response, anxiety, fear, and other nervous disorders in the traumatized individual. The dogs encourage quicker bonding between the clinician and the client. This bonding is the foundation of the client/clinician relationship. It allows the client to feel secure and safe, which lowers fear of judgement and improves health and treatment success.
